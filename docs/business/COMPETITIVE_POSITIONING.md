# Competitive positioning

## Category
Meet is not merely an event calendar, friendship swipe app or fixed-format dinner service. Positioning: **activity/event-first social participation layer for real cities**.

## Competitive sets
- event discovery/organizing: Meetup, event/ticket platforms, municipal/event directories;
- structured social experiences: Meet5, Timeleft and similar services;
- friendship/community: Commu, Frendie, Bumble BFF-like products and local groups;
- substitutes: Instagram/TikTok/Facebook groups, WhatsApp/Telegram communities, Google search/maps and direct venue channels.

## Win thesis
Meet should win when a user wants: `what can I do nearby?` + `who is going / can I join?` + `help me actually participate safely` in one flow across many physical-event sources.

## Non-goals for differentiation
Do not differentiate through needless infrastructure, generic AI features, public popularity scores, random DMs or dating mechanics.

## Feature copy test
Pods, attendee UI or recommendation widgets are copyable. Defensible advantage must come from city liquidity, event/attendance outcome data, trust, organizer relationships and distribution.

## Competitor research requirements
For each meaningful competitor maintain dated evidence for: target cohort, geographic strength, supply model, social mechanics, pricing, organizer model, safety, retention hooks, acquisition channels and observed weaknesses. Re-check before roadmap decisions; competitor URLs alone are not evidence.

## Strategic rejection criteria
If users primarily value passive event browsing and social/attendance mechanics do not improve repeat behaviour, Meet risks becoming an undifferentiated aggregator. Product strategy must then change rather than defending the original thesis by adding features.
