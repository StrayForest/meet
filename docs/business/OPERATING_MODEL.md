# Lean operating model — founder + AI, density before headcount

## Principle
Meet should use architecture and AI agents to increase capital efficiency, not to maximize code output. The scarce resources before PMF are founder attention, customer learning, local relationships and social liquidity.

## Founder/AI split
AI agents may accelerate implementation, tests, documentation, analysis and routine engineering. They do not replace accountable human judgment for security-critical changes, product strategy, customer interviews, partnerships, moderation policy or production-risk acceptance.

Agent-generated code is governed by the same architecture fitness, contracts, tests, review and CI gates as human code. More generated code is not progress unless it closes a product/business milestone.

## Founder bottleneck
Treat `bus factor = 1` as an explicit company risk. Avoid making the founder simultaneously the permanent bottleneck for engineering, growth, partnerships, community operations and trust/safety.

## Early functional ownership
Before hiring, assign explicit hats even if one person owns several:
- product/technology;
- growth/community and customer discovery;
- organizer/supply partnerships;
- trust & safety / moderation operations;
- finance/legal/compliance.

Track where founder hours are spent. Repetitive work that does not require founder judgment should be automated, delegated or removed.

## First non-technical leverage
Because the engineering system is designed for high AI leverage, the first major human capacity gap may be growth/community/partnerships rather than another generalist developer. Hiring order follows measured bottlenecks, not startup convention.

## Capital-efficiency rule
`architecture ready` does not mean `every managed service activated` or `every feature implemented`.

Maintain interfaces/boundaries for future scale, but activate infrastructure, headcount and operational complexity only when required by launch, safety, reliability or measured workload.

## Weekly operating review
Until PMF evidence is strong, review at least:
1. successful and repeat IRL participation;
2. Helsinki liquidity by category/cohort/language;
3. customer interview findings and failed journeys;
4. acquisition channel → repeat-attendee economics;
5. organizer pipeline/willingness to pay;
6. safety/moderation incidents and load;
7. roadmap items that can be removed or deferred;
8. founder bottlenecks.

## Anti-patterns
- optimizing story points, commits or generated LOC;
- adding engineers to compensate for unclear product scope;
- activating expensive infrastructure for hypothetical scale;
- broad paid acquisition into weak local density;
- allowing technical work to crowd out organizer/community/customer work.
