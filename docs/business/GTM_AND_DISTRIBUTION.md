# GTM and distribution — city-first acquisition system

## Principle
Distribution is a product system. Do not scale paid traffic into an empty social graph. Nationwide product/event coverage can exist while **active growth remains Helsinki-first**.

## Channel portfolio
Track each channel with spend **and human-time cost**, qualified visits, activation, first intent, successful IRL participation, 30/60-day repeat and referral rate.

| Channel | Initial use | Main advantage | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO/public event pages | demand capture for event/category/city queries | compounding low-marginal-cost acquisition | directory traffic may not convert socially |
| organizers/venues | supply + audience distribution | both marketplace sides from one relationship | slow sales/low organizer motivation |
| expat/newcomer communities | dense early English-speaking cohort | high social need | community fragmentation |
| Finnish local communities | mainstream city demand | local legitimacy | crowded channels |
| Russian-speaking communities | dense launch-language cohort | strong local network wedge | must not position Meet as language-exclusive |
| universities/employers/relocation partners | newcomer acquisition | recurring cohorts | partnership sales cycle |
| creator/community hosts | seed social proof/Pods | human trust and density | operational dependency |
| referrals/invites | endogenous growth | strongest network-effect signal | weak until core value exists |
| paid social/search | controlled experiments only | fast learning | can mask weak retention/CAC |

## Public-web/SEO funnel
Search/social link → genuinely useful public event/category/city page → privacy-safe social value → app/web activation → join/save → attendance → repeat/referral.

SEO is a first-class acquisition hypothesis, not a later marketing task. Build indexable city/category/event/organization surfaces only when they provide unique current value; avoid thin programmatic pages. Signup walls must not destroy search value.

Measure organic landing → successful IRL participation, not only rankings/traffic.

## Growth constraints
Do not increase a channel budget materially unless its cohort has credible downstream attendance/repeat data. Optimize CAC to **repeat successful IRL participant** as the mature consumer acquisition metric; use CAC to first successful IRL participant while cohorts are young. Never optimize primarily for install or registration.

## First 1,000-user playbook
Prefer manual/high-touch density building over broad media spend:
1. choose a small number of Helsinki categories/time windows with repeatable demand;
2. recruit organizers/venues/community hosts that can seed both supply and participants;
3. work directly with Finnish-, English- and Russian-speaking launch communities;
4. make sure priority opportunities are socially non-empty before sending broad traffic;
5. interview attendee, repeat, no-show and browse-only cohorts every week;
6. publish useful public event/category pages and measure organic conversion;
7. scale only channels/categories producing credible attendance and repeat.

The goal is not 1,000 registrations. It is enough concentrated users to observe repeated IRL behaviour and emerging endogenous social activity.

## Language strategy
`fi/en/ru` are all launch acquisition surfaces. Measure activation, attendance, repeat and cross-language participation separately. A language cohort that materially outperforms may become a temporary GTM wedge without changing Meet into a language-exclusive brand.

## Orchestration vs organic liquidity
Founder/community seeding is acceptable early. Track the share of successful participations that require manual intervention. Healthy progress means the same city/category increasingly works without founder-created social proof.

## Scale test
Before broad paid growth, require:
- H1 liquidity in the target cohort/category;
- credible H2–H4 downstream behaviour;
- manageable safety/support load;
- known CAC per successful/repeat participant;
- evidence that additional users improve rather than dilute local social value.
