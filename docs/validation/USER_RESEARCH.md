# User research protocol

## Purpose
Validate behaviour and decision friction before expanding the product. Research is paired with quantitative evidence; neither replaces the other.

## Required recurring cohorts
Interview/review samples from:
- discovered + attended + repeated;
- joined but no-showed;
- browsed but took no action;
- seriously considered an event but did not attend;
- wanted company / used company finding;
- explicitly did not want company finding;
- organizers/hosts with strong and weak outcomes.

## Core questions
Prefer concrete recent behaviour over hypothetical feature preference:
- What were you trying to do that day?
- Where would you normally look for an activity?
- What made this event worth considering?
- What almost stopped you from going?
- Were you planning to go alone? Did that matter?
- What did you need to know before arriving?
- What happened in the first five minutes after arrival?
- Did social context/company finding change your decision?
- Would you want to meet any participant again, and specifically for what action?
- What would make you use Meet again next week/month?

Avoid leading questions such as `Would you use a social graph?`.

## Accessibility and inclusion
Recruit across fi/en/ru, age ranges within the 18+ scope, different digital confidence, accessibility needs, newcomer/local contexts and different travel patterns. Do not infer sensitive traits that participants did not provide for the research purpose.

## Evidence labels
Every finding is tagged as one of:
- `MEASURED_BEHAVIOUR`
- `INTERVIEW_EVIDENCE`
- `USABILITY_OBSERVATION`
- `EXPERT_HYPOTHESIS`
- `SYNTHETIC_HYPOTHESIS`

Only the first three count as direct user evidence. Synthetic panels are useful for generating test cases, never percentages presented as real users.

## Weekly output
Record: cohort/cell, sample quality, top friction, contradictory evidence, changed assumption confidence, product decision, experiment to run next. Preserve negative evidence.
