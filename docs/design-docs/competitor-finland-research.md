# Competitor and Finland UX research — 2026-08

This is a pattern study, not permission to copy proprietary UI/assets.

## Meet5
Observed product patterns:
- activity-first bottom navigation and local activity list;
- filters including schedule, interests, age and gender;
- attendee context and group chat;
- small group emphasis; public/group context for safety;
- user-created activities.

Use:
- clear activity cards;
- attendee facepile/count as reassurance;
- small-group/capacity information;
- useful age/language filters.

Do not copy:
- member-directory emphasis or premium private-chat patterns that move the product toward person browsing.

Sources:
- https://www.meet5.com/
- https://faq.meet5.com/en/help/app-features
- https://faq.meet5.com/help/the-concept-of-meet5

## Timeleft
Observed:
- minimal choices;
- choose activity/date, book, show up;
- matching details/location progressively disclosed;
- warm, spacious visual design and strong single CTA;
- no swipe/bio planning burden in core flow.

Use:
- progressive disclosure;
- strong commitment CTA;
- reveal private/high-risk location only when appropriate;
- keep core flows short.

Do not copy:
- opaque matching as the main discovery model; Meet users need agency over public events.

Sources:
- https://timeleft.com/
- https://help.timeleft.com/hc/en-150/articles/28529250732444-What-is-Timeleft-and-How-Does-It-Work

## Meetup
Observed:
- search-led event discovery;
- quick date chips such as Today/Tomorrow/Weekend;
- category sections and map/list transition;
- event imagery, organizer and attendee context.

Use:
- fast temporal filters;
- category scanning;
- public event SEO/deep links.

Avoid:
- making persistent Groups/community hierarchy the first concept in V1.

## Commu — Finnish local reference
Observed:
- map-based local utility;
- in-app chat avoids exchanging personal contacts;
- strong identity verification is required for some sensitive help requests;
- 2026 redesign explicitly attempted to simplify the product.

Finnish App Store review signal: at least one user described the earlier experience as complex/difficult. Treat this as qualitative evidence, not a population-wide conclusion.

Use:
- map as a powerful secondary mode;
- contextual identity verification;
- local plain-language utility.

Avoid:
- map-first complexity and too many concepts on one surface.

Sources:
- https://apps.apple.com/fi/app/commu/id1568337013
- https://commuapp.fi/

## Frendie — Finnish local reference
Finnish App Store feedback exposes two risks directly relevant to Meet:
- inability to combine local/age filtering well enough for some users;
- users treating a friendship product like dating and fake-profile concerns.

Use:
- strong location + age/time/activity filters;
- explicit anti-dating policy and reporting;
- trust badges/verification.

Avoid:
- person-first discovery as core navigation.

Source:
- https://apps.apple.com/fi/app/frendie/id1630395278

## Finnish digital-design references
We do not claim a single national aesthetic preference. We use established Finnish digital systems as evidence for local expectations around clarity.

Helsinki Design System emphasizes consistent, accessible experiences and recommends minimizing navigation elements/clutter. Suomi.fi Design System emphasizes accessibility, usability, consistency and cognitive understandability.

Sources:
- https://hds.hel.fi/
- https://hds.hel.fi/patterns/navigation/examples/
- https://designsystem.suomi.fi/
- https://designsystem.suomi.fi/info/accessibility/

## Resulting Meet design hypothesis
- event feed is primary; map secondary;
- clean neutral surfaces + restrained nature-adjacent palette;
- high information clarity, low decoration;
- 24-hour time and locale-correct dates;
- large readable text and targets for broad age range;
- participant context visible without turning people into catalog cards;
- one dominant CTA;
- trust/safety information near join/host decisions.

Validate this hypothesis with Finnish usability testing before broad launch.
