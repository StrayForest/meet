# GTM and distribution — city-first acquisition system

## Principle
Distribution is a product system. Do not scale paid traffic into an empty social graph.

## Channel portfolio
Track each channel with spend/time cost, qualified visits, activation, first intent, successful IRL participation, 30-day repeat and referral rate.

| Channel | Initial use | Main advantage | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEO/public event pages | demand capture for event/category/city queries | compounding low-marginal-cost acquisition | directory traffic may not convert socially |
| organizers/venues | supply + audience distribution | both marketplace sides from one relationship | slow sales/low organizer motivation |
| expat/newcomer communities | dense early English-speaking cohort | high social need | community fragmentation |
| Finnish local communities | mainstream city demand | local legitimacy | crowded channels |
| Russian-speaking communities | dense launch-language cohort | strong local network wedge | must not position Meet as language-exclusive |
| universities/employers/relocation partners | newcomer acquisition | recurring cohorts | partnership sales cycle |
| creator/community hosts | seed social proof/Pods | human trust and density | operational dependency |
| referrals/invites | endogenous growth | strongest network-effect signal | weak until core value exists |
| paid social/search | controlled experiments only | fast learning | can mask weak retention/CAC |

## Public-web funnel
Search/social link → useful public event page → visible but privacy-safe social value → app/web activation → join/save → attendance → repeat/referral.

SEO pages must be genuinely useful without signup; signup walls must not destroy search value. Social proof shown publicly follows privacy policy and user settings.

## Growth constraints
Do not increase a channel budget materially unless its cohort has credible downstream attendance/repeat data. Optimize CAC to **successful IRL participant**, not install or registration.

## First 1,000-user playbook
Prefer manual/high-touch density building over broad media spend: recruit organizers/hosts, seed selected categories, partner with newcomer/language communities, ensure upcoming events are socially non-empty, interview early cohorts weekly, and measure every channel to attendance.
